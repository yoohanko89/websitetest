Olive green · The leaf

Green tea & matcha

A fresh leaf. Two distinct expressions.

Green tea and matcha begin with the tea plant, Camellia sinensis. For green tea, heat after picking limits oxidation; leaves are then shaped and dried. The result depends on cultivar, picking season and processing. A vivid leaf in the field does not tell the whole story of the finished tea.

Matcha is finely milled tea made from leaves grown with a shading stage and processed for grinding. Conventional matcha production uses tencha: steamed, dried leaves prepared without the rolling used for many other green teas, with stems and veins removed before milling. Not every green tea powder is matcha. Buyers should ask about shading, leaf preparation, milling and storage, then evaluate a sample in the intended drink or recipe.

Buyer notes

Ask about cultivar, harvest, processing, particle size, sensory profile and light-resistant packaging.

Gold yellow · The fruit

Yuzu / Yuja

Sunlight held in a fragrant peel.

Yuja is the Korean name for yuzu, Citrus junos. Its aromatic peel and tart juice bring different qualities to beverages, sauces and confectionery. Picture fruit on the branch: textured skin, green leaves and the moment before picking. Preserving that character depends on the handling after harvest.

Fresh fruit, juice, peel, puree and sweetened preparations are different buying specifications. Clarify whether a proposed ingredient includes sugar, seeds, peel or other additions. Compare acidity, soluble solids, processing method and storage conditions. For a formulated product, taste the sample at the actual usage level so that aroma, sweetness and bitterness can be judged together.

Buyer notes

Define the format, ingredient composition, acidity, soluble solids and intended application.

Burgundy · The root

Ginseng & red ginseng

From living soil to a considered process.

The lifted root is our image of beginnings: earth still clinging to its surface, fine roots intact. Korean ginseng refers to Panax ginseng. Fresh ginseng is the harvested root; red ginseng is produced by steaming and drying it. Red ginseng's darker appearance and distinctive flavor come from processing, rather than from a separate red-rooted plant.

Whole roots, slices, powders and extracts require different specifications. Ask for botanical identity, root age where documented, processing details and composition. For extracts, clarify the extraction method, concentration, carriers and any agreed analytical markers. Choose the form through application trials and documented requirements, rather than assuming that color or a photograph establishes quality.

Buyer notes

Confirm species, form, processing, composition and the documentation required for your market.

Our ingredient philosophy

Begin with the essential.
Make the details clear.

KO'S places the ingredient before the finished product: where it comes from, how it is grown, what happens at harvest and how it reaches the next maker. Freshness is a practical question about elapsed time, processing, packaging and storage. For a dried leaf or steamed root, careful preservation may matter more than a claim of being freshly picked.

Organic cultivation is an aspiration that must be supported by evidence when a product is sold as organic. Buyers should verify a current certificate, its product scope, the certified operator and acceptance in the destination market. The landscape imagery on this page does not establish certified-organic status. Product claims belong with the relevant documents for the proposed supply.

  1. 01

    Identity & origin

    State the ingredient, botanical species where relevant, required form and destination. Request lot origin and traceability records.

  2. 02

    Composition & performance

    Specify additions, processing, sensory targets and how the ingredient will be used. Evaluate samples in your own application.

  3. 03

  4. 04

    Commercial & logistics details

    Request current availability, sample arrangements, order quantities, lead time, packaging and shipment terms. Confirm import requirements before ordering.

A FEW USEFUL QUESTIONS

Know what
you are buying.

Is every green tea powder matcha?

No. Matcha involves specific cultivation and leaf preparation as well as milling. Ask how the leaves were shaded and processed, and evaluate the powder against your application requirements.

Are yuzu and yuja the same ingredient?

Yuja is the Korean name for yuzu. A fresh fruit and a sweetened yuja preparation have different compositions, so always check the format and full ingredient list.

What is the difference between ginseng and red ginseng?

Fresh ginseng is the harvested root. Red ginseng is ginseng that has been steamed and dried. Processing, format and composition should be specified separately when buying.

Are the ingredients shown certified organic?

This page describes an ingredient philosophy and growing landscapes. It does not certify a product. Confirm current organic documentation and its applicability to the exact product and destination market.

What should I include in a sourcing inquiry?

Share your target ingredient and format, intended application, destination country, estimated quantity and required documents. Availability, origin, pricing and delivery terms require a separate confirmed offer.
